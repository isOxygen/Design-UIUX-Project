# Seven-minute rehearsal

| Time | Slide | What to say or do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:50 | Checking usage and getting connected | Open with two returning-subscriber goals: check what is left or set up another device. |
| 0:50–1:45 | What does a returning subscriber need? | Explain the audience and the scope. The scenario is provisional, not interview-derived. |
| 1:45–3:15 | Status and Manage have different jobs | Explain what each tab does: Status shows data, time, and validity; Manage groups the two connection actions. |
| 3:15–4:15 | Why use tabs? | Compare the one-page view and a shortcut. The tabs add a switch when changing goals but separate information and actions. |
| 4:15–5:35 | Demo | Start from Status, choose Manage, open Get config, return, and open Subscription link. Show only demo values. Mention both upgrade options as secondary, not as the flow. |
| 5:35–7:00 | What feedback supported the split? | Explain the positive informal feedback from live users. Say why the split worked for the design: monitoring stays focused, and multiple connection methods live together on Manage. Less scrolling is a secondary benefit. Present the feedback as qualitative, not as a performance measurement. |
